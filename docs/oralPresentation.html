4  Oral presentation

An oral presentation is essentially a spoken form of technical writing; it involves both the activity and the art of speaking to the public. With the spread of modern media and technology, oral presentation has become a significant means of communication for scientists, engineers and other technical personnel. The presentation situation rests on interaction between the presenter and the audience. Because it brings the listener face to face with the presenter, an oral presentation also broadens the thinking of those who wrote the underlying report or project. It is therefore important for every presenter to acquire the skills of oral presentation. This chapter builds on the general principles of Chapter 3.

4.1 Features of an oral presentation

  • It is a participative, two-way communication process.
  • It is purposeful, interactive and audience oriented.
  • It communicates a message in a way that brings about the desired change in the audience’s understanding and opinion.

4.2 How to make a good oral presentation

4.2.1 Conquering stage fright

Stage fright arises mainly because most of us are not accustomed to speaking in public. Its signs include trembling hands, excessive sweating and loss of fluency. To overcome it:

  • prepare the subject thoroughly;
  • do some warm-up exercises;
  • eat a healthy meal and drink enough water;
  • rehearse the presentation.

4.2.2 Knowing the audience

Before planning the presentation, consider:

  • the age group of the audience;
  • the needs of the audience;
  • the usefulness of the presentation to them;
  • the information they should gain after attending it.

4.2.3 Content of the presentation

  • It should not be too difficult to understand.
  • If it is too long, shorten it to fit the time available.
  • Include examples and illustrations to support the argument.

4.2.4 Structure of the presentation

A typical presentation has three parts: the beginning, the middle and the end (introduction, body and conclusion).

The beginning (introduction)

The beginning is the most important part of a presentation, so be confident and well prepared for it.

  • Greet the audience: “Hello”, “Good morning”, and so on.
  • Introduce yourself, for example: “Good afternoon, ladies and gentlemen. Let me introduce myself…” or “Good morning, everyone. I’d like to start by introducing myself. My name is…”
  • Give the title and introduce the subject, using phrases such as “I plan to speak about…”, “Today I’m going to talk about…”, “The subject of my presentation is…”, “The theme of my talk is…” or “I’ve been asked to give you an overview of…”.
  • Give a brief outline of the main points you will cover.

The middle (body)

  • Provide detailed information about the topic.
  • Give clear explanations.
  • Provide enough evidence to be convincing.
  • Use analogies and stories to explain complicated ideas and to reinforce key points.

The end (conclusion)

  • Summarise your points.
  • Conclude the topic with the ideas developed in the talk.
  • End with “Thank you.”
  • If you are taking questions, tell the audience: “I’ll now be happy to answer any questions.”

4.2.5 Delivery of the presentation

Quality of voice

  • Adjust your volume and tone to the size of the audience.
  • Use a microphone or collar microphone where needed.
  • Pause at appropriate points.
  • Pronounce difficult words clearly and correctly.

Rapport with the audience

  • Make eye contact with the audience.
  • Shift your gaze from one person to another so that you take in everyone.
  • Involve the audience by asking questions.

Body language

  • Express confidence through your smile and movements.
  • Stand straight, and do not stay rooted to one spot.
  • Avoid unnecessary hand movements.
  • Dress formally.

Effective use of visual aids

  • Keep the layout pleasant and easy to read; a horizontal (landscape) layout is preferable.
  • Use clear fonts such as Arial, Helvetica, Times New Roman, Futura, Optima, Verdana, New Century Schoolbook or Courier. Some organisations prescribe a particular style.
  • Use a font size of 20 or more, depending on the size of the room. A useful scheme is 20 points for main headings, 16 for subheadings and 14 for other text.
  • Use capital letters, bold face, italics, underlining, reverse type (white on black) or shading to highlight key points, but sparingly.
Practical assignment

Prepare a ten-minute oral presentation with slides on a topic of your choice from biotechnology. Record the opening sentences you will use for the greeting, self-introduction and outline, and the closing sentences for the summary and invitation to questions.

  • Stage fright → prepare, warm up, eat and drink well, rehearse.
  • Audience → age group, needs, usefulness, expected learning.
  • Structure → introduction (greet, introduce, title, outline) → body (detail, evidence, stories) → conclusion (summary, thanks, questions).
  • Delivery → voice, rapport, body language, visual aids.
  • Slides → landscape layout, clear fonts, 20/16/14 point sizes.
