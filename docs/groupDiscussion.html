1  Group discussion

Group discussion (GD) is a comprehensive technique used to judge the suitability of individuals for admission, scholarships, jobs and similar opportunities. A GD assesses the overall personality of an individual (thoughts, feelings and behaviour) while working in a group. A topic is presented to the group members for discussion, and while the discussion goes on, a panel of observers watches the participants. Through this observation the panel judges the intellectual, social, leadership and communication skills of each candidate.

1.1 Importance of group discussion

Admission to many universities and business schools, campus placements after graduation, and recruitment to multinational companies all look for two things: knowledge of the field, and the ability to work in a group, either as a leader or as a member. A group discussion is a very effective way of judging the second of these, the candidate’s ability to work with others.

1.2 Skills judged in a group discussion

The panel observing a group discussion usually looks for:

  • how well you communicate with others;
  • how easily you behave and interact with the other members of the group;
  • how open-minded you are;
  • how flexible or rigid you are in accepting the viewpoints of others;
  • your leadership skills;
  • your analytical abilities;
  • your problem-solving and critical thinking skills;
  • your time management skills;
  • your ability to put forth ideas in an unbiased manner;
  • your social attitude and confidence.

1.3 Components of a group discussion

Purpose

The first component is the purpose of holding the discussion. The aim of a group discussion is to arrive at a satisfactory conclusion, and a GD usually helps the group decide the next course of action.

Planning

Planning is a crucial element. A group discussion that aims to arrive at a decision cannot be a random expression of ideas; it has to be planned so that every step takes the group closer to its goal.

Participation

Participation of the members is the life of any group discussion. Every member has to be drawn into the discussion, and it should not be dominated by a few.

1.5 Preparing for a group discussion

Nobody knows in advance what the topic of a GD will be, so it is wise to keep yourself informed on a broad range of subjects.

  1. Current affairs. Read newspapers regularly and follow the news. Understand the current crises facing the world, recent development initiatives, relations between countries, and similar issues.
  2. Historical topics. Have a fair knowledge of the history of your own country and of other countries. The topic may not come directly from history, but historical information helps you cite examples and make references when needed.
  3. Sports, arts and literature. Keep a general awareness of major events, personalities and works in these areas.
  4. Data. Familiarise yourself with important figures and statistics. A relevant number used at the right moment creates a strong impression on the assessors.

Read as widely as possible and build sound knowledge on many topics; documentaries help here. Improve your vocabulary, not so that you can use heavy words, but so that you understand topics better and contribute effectively. Finally, picture yourself succeeding in the discussion; confidence comes from preparation.

Practical assignment

Write down the points you would raise in a group discussion on the topic “Indian education during the COVID-19 pandemic”. Organise them as an opening statement, three or four main points with supporting facts, and a concluding statement.
